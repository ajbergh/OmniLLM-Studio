## Motion Design mode

### What is Motion Design mode?

Motion Design is an editing mode for animated compositions. It uses the same timeline as the nonlinear editing views but focuses the right rail into **Design**, **Animate**, **Effects**, **AI**, and **Export**, keeping visual layout separate from time-based motion.

### What are animation blocks?

Animation blocks are named In, During, and Out behaviors that compile into ordinary, editable keyframes. The shared registry contains 21 behaviors, including fades, pop/reveal treatments, blur reveals, float, Ken Burns, and blur-out treatments. The blocks are a quick authoring method—not a separate rendering format.

### What happens if I apply a block more than once?

Applying a block in the same family replaces the keyframes previously generated by that family. Shift-click to intentionally stack another block. Each block stores provenance for its generated keyframes, so it can be replaced, removed, or undone without removing manual keyframes you authored.

### Can I turn a template into my own composition?

Yes. The starter library includes 12 remixable motion compositions: App Demo, Product Hero, Floating Screens, Feature Carousel, Logo Reveal, Social Quote, Podcast Clip, Vertical Product Ad, Lower Third Pack, Before / After, Cinematic Title, and Photo Slideshow. Replace a template slot's media without losing the slot's timing, animation, effects, or camera data, then edit any generated property.

## Keyframes and curves

### Which properties can I keyframe?

Clip keyframes can animate position (`x`, `y`, `z`), scale (uniform or independent X/Y), rotation (Z and spatial X/Y), opacity, volume, and the amount of effects present on that clip. Scene cameras have their own keyframes for position, spatial rotation, field of view, and focus depth.

### Are animation-block keyframes editable?

Yes. Generated keyframes are normal timeline keyframes; change their value, timing, and interpolation in the inspector or keyframe lane. The animation-block record is retained only to make block replacement/removal predictable.

### Which easing options are available?

Use linear, ease-in, ease-out, ease-in-out, step, cubic Bezier, or spring curves. Bezier controls are explicit (`x1`, `y1`, `x2`, `y2`); spring controls include stiffness, damping, and mass.

### Will a spring look the same in preview and export?

Yes within the renderer's documented sampling model. Bezier and spring curves are deterministically sampled for the preview/export fidelity expansion. Springs are segment-local and start with zero incoming velocity at each keyframe boundary, which keeps their behavior reproducible.

### Can I animate effects?

Yes. Add an effect to a clip, then keyframe its amount. The property is limited to an effect actually present on that clip, avoiding arbitrary or invalid effect paths.

## 2.5D, scenes, cameras, and effects

### What does 2.5D mean here?

Clips can have depth (`z`), independent X/Y scale, spatial rotations, anchor, and perspective in addition to standard 2D position/scale/rotation. Layer order and `z_index` still determine stacking; depth becomes most visible when a scene camera moves to create parallax.

### What are scenes?

Scenes are non-overlapping time ranges that sit above the flat layer timeline. They let you organize a composition into shots without moving its clips into nested sequences. Scenes can be selected, duplicated, reordered, and used as snap targets, and may contain camera keyframes and scene-wide effects.

### What can the camera animate?

A scene-relative camera can animate X/Y/Z position, X/Y/Z rotation, field of view, and focus depth. Presets include push/pull, pan, crane, dolly, orbit, handheld drift, and rack focus. Use depth-separated clips with camera movement for parallax.

### Which effects are available?

The effect browser identifies runtime support. Available clip/scene effects include blur, brightness, contrast, saturation, grayscale, shadow, background blur, chroma key, sharpen, vignette, film grain, bloom, color grade, edge fade, RGB split, ghost trail, motion blur, depth of field, and rack focus.

### Are all effects identical in preview and export?

No; treat the effect browser badges and the render capability report as authoritative. FFmpeg exports the documented color/cinematic effects and camera motion. Drop shadow and background blur remain unsupported by export; chroma key is rendered by FFmpeg but does not map exactly to CSS preview; true X/Y tilt is a deliberate partial 3D approximation.

### How do I know whether a feature will render?

Check the effect/transition export-support badge and run the pre-render validation checklist. The renderer deliberately reports partial or unsupported features rather than silently claiming frame-perfect output. Details are in the [rendering guide](VIDEO_RENDERING.md#known-previewexport-differences).
